Connect Key Foundation

Founder & President, 501(c)(3) Youth Nonprofit

Connect Key Foundation is the 501(c)(3) youth nonprofit I founded and now run across 17+ regions, including Southern California, Georgia, and Hong Kong. I direct 150+ volunteers in five divisions — STEM, art, English, outreach, and environment — who have delivered more than 50,000 hours of free service to their communities.

150+Volunteers
50,000+Service Hours
17+Regions
$5,000STEM Action Grant

We put CurioCrate STEM kits into students’ hands, run free labs at the YMCA, and bring therapeutic art and conversation to patients at PIH Health and Miller Children’s & Women’s Hospital.

Two published books turned what our volunteers were hearing in the field into something readers could hold.

“Dear World: Now We Speak” and “Korea: The Heart and Art” joined a $5,000 Society for Science STEM Action Grant in letting us reach further every year. This year we also directed LA wildfire disaster relief efforts and led senior outreach visits across Cerritos and Fullerton.

It started on a call with friends that ran way past 1am, complaining about how repetitive most volunteering felt — showing up to do work nobody really needed help with. So we started calling elementary schools instead. I still think about one of our shyest students tugging my shirt to ask what we were doing next week, and another kid yelling “bye, science guy!” on his way out. That’s the part that keeps me doing this, not the volunteer count.

Art

Scholar & Volunteer, Ryman Arts

A competitive portfolio review admitted me to Ryman Arts, where I study watercolor, graphite, acrylic, and charcoal as a three-time scholarship recipient.

The part that matters most is giving it back: every week I coordinate 12+ volunteers to lead free art sessions for elderly residents, turning studio practice into something shared.

My work has earned 3rd place in the City of Cerritos Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Day Art Contest and been featured in the City of Norwalk’s “The Walk” art exhibit.

+Studio session

Winning is nice, but the weekly sessions with residents are the part I’d keep even without a scholarship attached to it.

Research

Student Researcher, Cal Poly Pomona — Dr. K. Anderson Lab

Under a faculty mentor at Cal Poly Pomona, I develop an optimized magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) engine geometry — running CFD simulations, applying machine learning tools, and building 3D models by hand.

2ndLACSEF Place
SilverKSEF Int’l Prize

My study of MHD thrusters for small-scale underwater vehicles is putting the work in front of researchers beyond my lab. More papers, posters, and project files are on the way.

I like this project for a different reason than the results on it — it’s the first thing I’ve worked on where nobody already knows the answer, including the person mentoring me.

Haegeum

Founder & President, Korean Traditional Music Institute of America

I founded the Korean Traditional Music Institute of America to share Korean traditional music with American audiences, and our ensemble has given 50+ performances.

Solo haegeum at Weill Recital Hall, Carnegie Hall — then 1st Prize at the World Competition, seven years running.

I won 1st Prize at the World Competition of Korean Traditional Performing Arts every year from 2017 to 2024 — plus the American Protege International Music Talent Competition in 2024, 2025, and 2026.

Carnegie Hall makes the highlight reel. What doesn’t is the years of weekly lessons before that, for an instrument almost nobody outside a small community still plays.

Civil Air Patrol

Cadet Commander, U.S. Air Force Auxiliary

As Cadet Commander I lead and develop 120+ cadets in one of California’s largest squadrons. I rebuilt our administration from the ground up and I’m driving the unit toward the Quality Unit Award.

120+Cadets Led
30+Ceremonies
300+Cadets at Encampment

With Color and Honor Guard I’ve presented the colors at official ceremonies statewide, and I was selected for summer encampment leadership staff, running logistics across the California Wing.

Early on, everything looked fine on paper and I still felt like I was fighting the squadron instead of leading it. What actually changed things was stepping back — handing my first sergeant the lesson plans and promotion boards I used to keep for myself, and staying quiet through her shaky first inspection instead of jumping in to fix it. She runs entire training blocks without me now. That’s the actual measure, not the ceremonies.

Computer Science Club

President, Gretchen Whitney High School

As president I teach 30+ underprivileged students Python, Java, and data structures, and guide club members preparing for hackathons, USACO, and their GitHub portfolios.

I carry hands-on lessons into partner elementary schools and local nonprofits, widening access to computer science for younger kids.

The comparison I keep coming back to isn’t another CS class, it’s card magic. Both start with the same question: what effect am I trying to create, and what’s the simplest way there? Teaching someone their first program has the same rush as teaching someone their first sleight of hand — watching them get why it works, then wanting to go build their own.

Free Flight

World Champion Team, National Youth Aviation Academy

I rank 3rd nationally in the F1A free flight division, and I was part of the World Champion Team at the 2026 FAI Junior World Championship in North Macedonia, competing against world cup champions.

Hand-launched, hand-built, timed to the second — then it flew us to a world title.

In 2025 I also took 1st at the North America World Championship, Arizona FF Championships, and Kotuku Cup, and 2nd at Maxmen International. I design, launch, and optimize hand-powered F1 aircraft for precision timed flight.

3rdNational Rank, F1A
5Podium Finishes, 2025–26

Most of what I know about this sport came from my coach, mid-meltdown after a bad practice: “There’s no such thing as your day. Focus up and look at the plane.” At Jr. Worlds the wind was some of the worst I’ve flown in — planes exploding in the air, crashing into tents. We still caught thermals on 20 of 23 launches. That’s the whole sport: you don’t get a bad day, you just watch closer.
